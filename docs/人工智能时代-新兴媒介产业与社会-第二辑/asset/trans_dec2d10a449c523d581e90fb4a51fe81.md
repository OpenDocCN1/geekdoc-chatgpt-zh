
# Empirical Study on Factors Influencing Agricultural Technicians' Media Use Ability

## Authors
Wu Zhiyuan, Yu Sijie

## Abstract
In the era of mass media, research on agricultural innovation diffusion did not pay particular attention to the role of media and media use ability. However, in the Internet era, social media has brought a breakthrough to agricultural innovation diffusion, especially in improving the efficiency of agricultural technology extension. This study conducted a questionnaire survey of 951 grassroots agricultural technology extension personnel in Hubei and used `SPSS 22` for statistical analysis. The results show that social media, including QQ and WeChat, is becoming an important tool for agricultural technology extension. However, the media use ability of grassroots agricultural technology extension personnel is not optimistic. This study found that stimulating agricultural technicians' perceived demand for advanced agricultural technology, increasing organizational emphasis, increasing agricultural technicians' media contact intensity, and enhancing their cultural capital and social capital all help improve their media use ability. This study contributes to the relatively weak research link in the traditional field of agricultural innovation diffusion—research on media roles. It also provides new ideas for improving the key qualities of grassroots agricultural technology extension personnel.

## Keywords
Agricultural innovation diffusion, Media contact, Media use ability

## I. Research Origin

Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations theory originated from the study of mass media effects and eventually became a classic theory in the study of mass communication effects[^1]. Therefore, since its inception, the theory of innovation diffusion has been closely linked with media technology.

Rogers proved the role of mass media in the "knowledge" stage of innovation diffusion[^2]177. Zhu Jianhua, He Zhou, and others believe that in promoting innovation diffusion, the combination of mass media and interpersonal communication achieves good results. Both mass media and interpersonal communication function in innovation diffusion by helping potential adopters form or change their subjective perceptions of innovation attributes[^3].

Of course, in the era dominated by mass media, media variables in innovation diffusion research struggled to become a focus. This is mainly because in the mass media era, mass media was highly monopolized by capital and interest groups[^4], and the general public, including agricultural technology extension personnel, could not control mass media tools and found it difficult to actively use mass media. As Rogers stated, in innovation diffusion, mass media is only a window for opinion leaders to access external new knowledge[^2]323. Researchers were also unable to measure the specific magnitude of the role played by mass media in agricultural innovation diffusion.

In the Internet and social media era, this situation is being broken: due to the rapid popularization of the Internet, the degree of social media diffusion exceeds that of all other media that have existed in history. The influence of social media is ubiquitous, with its tentacles extending even to the most remote corners of rural areas[^5].

The rapid popularization of social media means individuals' mastery of media tools, granting them increasing initiative. Relying on social media, individuals can have more opportunities for participation and communication; engage in dialogue and interaction with more people; share knowledge with more people; have more opportunities to publicly express their views and ideas; participate in or form communities and groups with shared interests; and connect with more strangers, groups, and originally unknown worlds through social media[^6]. Obviously, these capabilities endowed to individuals by social media are highly beneficial for grassroots agricultural technology extension personnel to improve their working conditions.

The evolution of media has also stimulated researchers' interest. They follow the evolution of media technology and forms to further study the role of media in innovation diffusion. Research finds that with the evolution of media technology, the role played by media in innovation diffusion becomes increasingly stronger, and its status increasingly prominent[^7].

From these studies, it is not difficult to conclude that the more media technologies agricultural technology extension personnel master, the stronger their media use ability, the more significant their achievements in promoting agricultural innovation diffusion.

Therefore, this study will further explore the following two questions: What is the current status of agricultural technology extension personnel's media use ability? What factors influence their media use ability? Obviously, clarifying these issues is highly beneficial for taking targeted measures in the next step to improve the working ability of agricultural technology extension personnel.

Media use ability, as an important outcome variable in this study, is defined based on Potter's theory of media literacy. James Potter believes that media use ability, as an important connotation of modern media literacy, has become a manifestation of individual modernity. Media use ability can be reflected in gaining control over the process of media influence, including both the ability to master media technology and the ability to process media information or content[^8]329.

Based on an instrumental perspective, this study operationalizes the definition of media use ability as the ability to master media technology. The other layer of meaning of media use ability—"the ability to process media information and content"—is not the object of investigation for the time being. Other theoretical foundations involved in this study include Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations theory and Bourdieu's social capital theory.

To deeply investigate agricultural technicians' media use ability, from over 100 counties and cities in Hubei, a stratified, then random, then cluster sampling method was adopted to select 200 townships in 12 counties and cities as samples. Using the questionnaire survey method, 951 agricultural technicians were visited to understand their media contact situations, individual capital endowments, and relevant demographic variables. Meanwhile, `SPSS` statistical software was used to process the collected data.

Through quantitative analysis, this study identified the main factors influencing agricultural technology extension personnel's media use ability, including: degree of contact with media, scale of individual social capital, and degree of craving for advanced agricultural technology.

## II. Theoretical Preparation

### 1. Innovation Diffusion Is Inseparable from Media Roles

"The essence of innovation diffusion is the interactive social process of people's subjective evaluation of new things."[^2]IX As the proposer and tireless optimizer of the theory of innovation diffusion, Rogers believes that the theory of innovation diffusion can explain the most important development process of human beings—social change.

From Rogers' interpretation of the essence of innovation diffusion, several key concepts can be grasped: "new things," "subjective evaluation," and "interaction." Grasping these keywords allows innovation diffusion to be viewed as a "process of information dissemination and communication about new things." In Rogers' own words, "Diffusion is a kind of communication, except that the content transmitted is about innovation."[^2]13 The key to diffusion is that one user shares information with other users, which requires the use of communication channels.

Therefore, in Rogers' innovation diffusion model, there are four main elements: "innovation," "communication channels," "time," and "social system"[^2]13. Rogers counted that in all diffusion research results, research on communication channels accounted for 7%[^2]98. Rogers defines communication channels as: "the means and methods by which information is transmitted from one party to another."[^2]20

Furthermore, Rogers believes: "Mass communication is the most effective channel for transmitting innovation information." The mass communication channels he refers to include radio, television, and newspapers. He cites Paul Lazarsfeld's research results, saying, "Innovative ideas often flow from radio and print media to opinion leaders, and then from opinion leaders to those groups less active toward innovation"[^2,9]. Later, Wilbur Schramm further elaborated on the views of Rogers, Lazarsfeld, and others in *Men, Messages and Media: A Look at Human Communication*, saying that in innovation diffusion, the influence of mass media is greater in the early stages of diffusion[^10].

The above background helps explain why media should be focused on in innovation diffusion research. Because the innovation diffusion process cannot do without communication channels. And media is a more effective communication channel than interpersonal communication and other channels.

Of course, Lazarsfeld, Rogers, Schramm, and others did not live to deeply experience the power of the Internet and new media, especially the role of social media in communication. Otherwise, they would certainly have new understandings of the role of media tools in innovation diffusion. However, systematic research on the effects of new media technologies often begins only after the technology has been accepted by the public to a considerable degree[^11]211. It can be expected that the social media era provides new opportunities for research in the field of innovation diffusion.

From the above, it can be seen that focusing on the role of media is a traditional perspective in innovation diffusion research.

The second reason for focusing on media roles in agricultural innovation diffusion is determined by the media's own functions.

McLuhan believes, "It is the medium, not the message, that determines changes in human behavior"[^11]226. McLuhan believes that contrary to common understanding, media is not only a carrier of knowledge and content, but also has a strong reaction on knowledge and content. Media has positivity and agency; it determines the structural mode and clarity of information, thus having a significant impact on information[^12]15. That is to say, the media itself also influences and shapes people's behavioral patterns. The impact of media on innovation diffusion is not limited to this; it also has a strong impact on the perception of innovation diffusion recipients. Different media act on different senses; print media affects vision, audio-visual media affects touch[^12]15.

Regarding the role of media, Charles Sterin further summarizes: The technological progress of mass media allowed humans in the early 21st century to discover themselves and brought about human evolution; people acquired the ability to effectively process the ever-increasing large amount of information simultaneously. Sterin also believes that with the development and progress of technology, the structure and values of society and culture will inevitably change. The emergence of each new medium creates new ways of social life and social behavior; media is the basic driving force of social development and also the hallmark distinguishing different social forms[^13]6. US research believes that media has a significant degree of influence in maintaining existing values, promoting the formation of new concepts, and changing original values[^14]. Entering the network media era, new media forms emerge endlessly. Nicholas Negroponte even asserted in his book *Being Digital* that in the digital media era, media is no longer information. Media will become a way of life, and this is true for everyone[^15].

According to the perspective of technological determinism, it can be deduced that the role of media in innovation diffusion will include two aspects: one is transmitting innovation information and knowledge; the other is playing a reverse role on innovation information and knowledge.

The third reason for focusing on media roles in agricultural innovation diffusion comes from research on the effects produced by media use.

James Potter's research shows that media tools can produce immediate effects and long-term effects on individuals and organizations. Immediate effects include cognitive effects, attitudinal effects, emotional effects, physiological effects, and behavioral effects. Among them, cognitive effects contain short-term learning, reinforcement learning, and expansive learning; attitudinal effects contain shaping views, changing views, contrast effects, inoculation, and immediate reinforcement; emotional effects contain immediate reactions and mood management; behavioral effects contain attraction, imitation, and activation. Long-term effects include mastering learning agendas, enhancing memory, inducing the essence of phenomena, exposing secrets of social operation, maintaining attention and interest, and changing cognitive behaviors[^8]354. Obviously, Potter's analysis helps further understand the value of media in innovation diffusion.

Not only that, according to the Uses and Gratifications theory, regarding specific media, different (or similar) media can satisfy different (or similar) needs. Elihu Katz, Jay Blumler, Michael Gurevitch, and others, in their famous 1974 paper "Utilization of Mass Communication by the Individual," pointed out that different media have different technical characteristics and information functions. For example, books and newspapers are related to self-realization and self-satisfaction; they help individuals "connect with self"; at the same time, newspapers, radio, and television help people connect with society and others[^16]. Obviously, the emergence of the Internet and social media not only possesses all the above functions of traditional media but also opens up infinite possibilities.

The fourth reason for focusing on media roles in agricultural innovation diffusion comes from the reality of rural socio-economic development. As mentioned earlier, in essence, innovation diffusion is the process of knowledge diffusion aided by media. In agricultural innovation diffusion practice, media is both the carrier of content and an important environmental factor for diffusion and dissemination. This has been fully reflected in the innovation diffusion practices of various countries. For example, in South Korea's "One Village, One Community" movement, newspapers successfully mobilized all sectors to pay attention to rural areas and undertook the important role of urban-rural communication channels. Radio played an important role in providing market information, weather forecasts, new crop varieties, cultivation techniques, and eliminating loneliness and isolation for the US agricultural population. In Japan's "One Village, One Product" movement, radio, television, and newspapers played a resonance effect, jointly promoting the "One Village, One Product" movement[^17].

Based on the above analysis, in the study of the agricultural innovation diffusion process, focusing on the use of media tools and their effects has become a matter of course.

Hence, the first question raised in this study is: In agricultural innovation diffusion, which media are the most commonly used or most valued by agricultural technology extension personnel?

### 2. The Importance of Media Use Ability Is Increasingly Prominent

The previous discussion elaborated on the increasing importance of media roles in agricultural innovation diffusion. Of course, the exertion of media roles relies on people to complete. Different individuals using media will obviously have different effects, because individuals' media use abilities differ.

The "Process School" believes that communication is the transmission of information, requiring high attention to the effect and correctness of communication. And this effect and correctness stem from how the sender and receiver encode and decode, and how the information transmitter uses the media[^18]. In other words, the effect of communication is related to the level of media use ability of both communication parties. Therefore, this study takes media use ability as a key outcome variable for investigation.

So-called media use ability is a sub-concept contained in the concept of media literacy. James Potter defines media literacy as "a perspective that we actively use to expose ourselves to media and interpret the meaning of the messages we encounter." Potter regards individuals' goals and motivations, individuals' skills, and individuals' knowledge structures as the three cornerstones of media literacy. The relationship among the three is that the skills mastered by individuals are tools, and information from media and the real world are raw materials. Tools and raw materials are used to construct our knowledge structures[^8]13-19. The media skills mentioned by Potter are similar to the media use ability mentioned in this study.

It should be seen that in Potter's era, media types were relatively limited. Therefore, his understanding of media use ability stopped at the analysis, evaluation, classification, induction, deduction, extraction, and synthesis of media content. In fact, the development of media technology exceeds ordinary people's imagination. The evolution of Internet technology over the past 20 years and the constantly emerging new media forms have been dazzling. For a person to simultaneously master multiple media proficiently is by no means an easy task. Therefore, media use ability should also include the mastery of different media technologies.

Combined with Marshall McLuhan's view, the medium is the message itself; what media brings to people is not only information and knowledge, but media technology itself also determines people's thinking patterns. It can be said that the rise of the Internet and social media, and the changes they bring to people's thinking patterns, the rise of Internet thinking, again verified the correctness of McLuhan's view. In this sense, every additional media technology an individual masters adds a thinking pattern.

Therefore, the mastery of media use technology should also be a focus of media use ability investigation. Obviously, the more types of media a person can skillfully use, the stronger their media use ability, meaning they have more choices and freedom in the information age.

For a long time, rural areas have been relatively closed in terms of information, with narrow information channels. Other channels, such as libraries and other infrastructure, various industry exchange meetings, are difficult to expand in a short time. The popularization of the Internet and the emergence of various new media make media use ability appear especially important. For agricultural technicians, this is even more so.
