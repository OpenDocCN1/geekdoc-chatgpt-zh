[6] There is a similar concept in quantum physics: the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle states that one cannot simultaneously know the exact position and momentum of a particle; the analogy here is that one cannot simultaneously know the exact price of a stock at a specific point in time. The different prices of the same stock on different exchanges are like Schrödinger's cat—value exists in superposition. The problem with high-frequency trading is that, unlike quantum physics, exchanges do not use observation of price at a specific time to collapse inconsistent prices (wave functions) into a single value; only when a trade occurs is there a single value, and only trades can transmit value (energy). If in physics you could make infinite observations, you could "cash out" by selecting superposed values, thereby harvesting energy for free—this is also a form of Maxwell's demon. In other words, exchanges give away information for free, while the real quantum world charges for information.
