

# Translator's Afterword

Even if the warp drive allows the *Enterprise* to soar through the cosmos, once a malfunction occurs, Chief Engineer Scott must still troubleshoot and repair it, or Captain Kirk has to fix it himself. At times like these, a fully functional intelligent robot would be ideal—at least it would spare fragile human bodies from venturing into danger. By comparison, the protagonist of *Interstellar* sees his happiness index skyrocket, both at work and in life, thanks to an assistant like TARS (that shape-shifting robot who cracks deadpan jokes), and humanity is ultimately saved because of it. J.A.R.V.I.S., created by Stark in *Iron Man*, is even more impressive. Wherever Stark goes, J.A.R.V.I.S. follows—from maintaining the Iron Man armor to handling daily affairs, it is like a shadow. Stark can obtain analytical results for all sorts of relevant information in the shortest possible time; he only needs to make the decisions. This ability to stay one step ahead of opponents at every turn is precisely the guarantee that Stark survives against the odds. Without J.A.R.V.I.S., Stark's combat effectiveness would be cut by at least half. Of course, from a certain perspective, AI capability isn't necessarily "the stronger, the better." Like *The Machine* and *Samaritan* in the TV series *Person of Interest*, these two highly self-evolving AIs eventually enter a world incomprehensible to humans. In their eyes, all of humanity is merely tools: experimental tools, combat tools, or just toys.

You might feel all this is worlds away, but this book uses detailed information and abundant facts to tell us that the era of "Synthetic Intelligence" and "Artificial Laborers" is arriving soon. From the perspective of human development, what kind of AI is most suitable?

First, it needs powerful information processing capabilities. It can perform data analysis on all relevant information as required and formulate plans with extremely high accuracy. Second, it is omnipresent—always there when you need it, appearing by your side in a suitable form at the first moment. Third, it is merely a competent assistant. It never makes decisions for you; it only offers suggestions and ideas. AI possessing these traits can make our lives simpler, more efficient, and safer.

In the near future, everyone can customize their own synthetic intelligence. You can delegate many life tasks to it. For instance, you can completely hand over driving duties to it, because it can efficiently fulfill your driving requirements, making the ride extremely smooth. For example, one day you have an emergency and need to reach a destination ASAP. You tell your "proxy driver." While en route, it secretly negotiates with other "proxy drivers": "Hey buddy, I've got an emergency, mind letting me through?" They coordinate, and your car zooms past—overtaking speed and timing flawless. You arrive at your destination early, smoothly and safely. There are many similar scenarios, but they share a common trait: these things get done without you even realizing it.

You can also upgrade your assistant in specific areas you favor based on your financial situation. For instance, if you are a gourmet, you can keep your custom artificial laborer at standard specs for other functions, but have the manufacturer upgrade a dedicated advanced culinary program. This way, you can enjoy professional-grade cuisine at home.

Perhaps your basic AI can't trade stocks for you and make money—after all, specialization exists; financial firms will own AIs akin to Formula 1 race cars, far surpassing your ordinary family sedan. But the basic version is perfectly adequate for daily life. Although your "proxy chef" might not cook Michelin three-star level dishes, surpassing the cooking skills of most humans in both variety and quality should be no problem.

What will future AI look like? Humanity's assistant, friend, or master? Please don't close your eyes to avoid this choice, because the future is about to begin, and the chance to choose may come only once.

These excellent books not only broaden horizons but also triggered Chinese people's thinking and discussion on technology and the future at a most opportune moment. ## How to Read Business Books

Business books differ from other types of books in reading purpose and method; therefore, they have specific reading principles and methods. Start by trying with one book, then apply them proficiently.

### Reading Principle 1: The 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

For business books, 80% of the essential value may reside in only 20% of the pages. Allocate reading time according to your reading ability.

### Reading Principle 2: Concentrated Effort Principle

Focus on breaking through the 20% core content within a specific time period. Alternatively, focus on conquering the reading of a single theme within a time period.

### Reading Principle 3: Progressive Principle

Efficient reading doesn't necessarily follow page order. You can pick parts that interest you, then expand from those interest points to other parts. Avoid greediness in reading business books. Start with a small theme, cultivate your reading ability, understand the writing style, argumentation methods, and case description techniques. The goal is to master the *method*—that is the most important.

### Reading Principle 4: "Teaching Others" Principle

Lead and control topics in your social circles, steering them toward your designed direction. This makes reading gains more solid, practical, and effective.

## Cultivating Reading Methods and Habits

1.  **Recall**. Business books are often not finished in one sitting. When picking up the book a second time, spend at least 15 minutes recalling the previous content—do not flip through it. Only look if you absolutely cannot remember. Train yourself strictly: you *must* recall. Persist 50 times, and it will gradually become a habit.

2.  **Take Notes**. Don't try to make notes highly logical or systematic; no need for profound insights. As long as there are words, it's brain exercise. Write and draw freely in margins—doodles, symbols, highlighting, bookmarks, sticky notes, dog-ears, even disassembling the book are all fine.

3.  **Reviews and PPTs**. Persisting in writing reviews significantly improves reading ability; making PPTs improves logical analysis. Start with reviews; after 5, try making PPTs. Do 5 PPTs consecutively, then write 3 more reviews. Persist, and reading ability will improve dramatically.

4.  **Transcending Thoughts**. Cultivating the above habits usually requires 6 months of strict training, completing at least 4 books. You will slowly find your thoughts breaking free, gaining a sense of surpassing the author. Comparing yourself to the author, surpassing them, attempting to think *above* the author—this is the inevitable result of improved reading ability.

Good methods are actually simple; the difficulty lies in execution. It requires willpower, persistence, and long-term consistency to form habits. Learn with your heart, and you will get a change of heart, a change of thought. Reading connects with thoughts.
